## Situation

Launch mass is 20 t, including a 1 t second stage.
First-phase mass cannot fall below 2 t. At separation, the second stage starts at 1 t;
the discarded mass is the pre-separation mass minus 1 t.

The second-stage engine has higher specific impulse (350 s versus 300 s) but much lower thrust (14.7 kN versus 294 kN).
The target is a 500 km circular orbit. Earth rotation is neglected, and acceleration is limited to 8 G.

## Formulation

### Coordinates

Use the [polar coordinates of Example 12](../12-rocket-ascent-polar-ssto/#coordinates).
Altitude is $R-R_e$, downrange distance is $R_e\theta$, and radial and tangential components are local vertical and horizontal components.

### Phases and linkage

At the optimized separation time $t_s$, position and velocity are continuous while mass resets:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R(t_s^+)&=R(t_s^-),&\theta(t_s^+)&=\theta(t_s^-),\\
V_r(t_s^+)&=V_r(t_s^-),&V_t(t_s^+)&=V_t(t_s^-),\\
m(t_s^+)&=1\ \mathrm t.
\end{aligned}
$$

Disable automatic mass continuity and impose the linkage explicitly
([linking phases](../../guide/usage/#linking-phases)).

### Dynamics

For stage $i=1,2$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot R&=V_r,&\dot\theta&=V_t/R,\\
\dot V_r&=(T_r-D_r)/m-\mu/R^2+V_t^2/R,\\
\dot V_t&=(T_t-D_t)/m-V_rV_t/R,\\
\dot m&=-\sqrt{T_r^2+T_t^2}/(g_0I_{sp,i}).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V=\sqrt{V_r^2+V_t^2},\qquad
D_r=\tfrac12\rho(h)V_rVC_{D,i}A_i,\qquad
D_t=\tfrac12\rho(h)V_tVC_{D,i}A_i,
$$

$$
\rho(h)=1.225e^{-h/8500},\qquad g(R)=\mu/R^2.
$$

The polar velocity terms express centrifugal acceleration and conservation of angular momentum.

### Boundary conditions

$$
R(0)=R_e,\quad\theta(0)=0,\quad V_r(0)=V_t(0)=0,\quad m(0)=20\ \mathrm t,
$$

$$
R(t_f)=R_e+500\ \mathrm{km},\qquad V_r(t_f)=0,\qquad V_t(t_f)=\sqrt{\mu/R(t_f)}.
$$

Target speed is about 7616.6 m/s. Final angle and time are free.

### Constraints

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&\ge R_e,\\
2\ \mathrm t\le m\le20\ \mathrm t&\quad\text{(phase 1)},\\
200\ \mathrm{kg}\le m\le1\ \mathrm t&\quad\text{(phase 2)},\\
\sqrt{T_r^2+T_t^2}&\le T_{\max,i},\\
-T_{\max,i}&\le T_r,T_t\le T_{\max,i},\\
\left|\frac{\boldsymbol T-\boldsymbol D}{m}\right|&\le8g_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Unlike Example 12, thrust components can be negative, allowing downward and backward thrust.
The second-stage mass lower bound is its 200 kg dry mass.
Acceleration excludes gravity.

### Objective and parameters

$$
J=-m(t_f)/(20\ \mathrm t).
$$

| Parameter | Stage 1 | Stage 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase-start mass | 20 t | 1 t |
| Mass lower bound | 2 t | 200 kg |
| $C_D,A$ | 0.2, 3.14 m² | 0.2, 3.14 m² |
| $I_{sp}$ | 300 s | 350 s |
| $T_{\max}$ | 294.2 kN | 14.7 kN |

Use $R_e=6371$ km, $g_0=9.80665$ m/s², and $\mu=3.986\times10^{14}$ m³/s².
Units follow Example 12 with mass scaled by 20 t.

### Initial guess

Use times $0\to100\to200$ s. Radius and angle rise cubically to target altitude and 25°.
Radial velocity is zero; tangential velocity increases linearly to target.
Mass decreases cubically from 20 to 2 t and from 1 t to 200 kg in the respective phases.
Radial and tangential thrust decrease cubically from 0.9 and 0.1 times each stage's maximum to zero.

## Explanation

### Throttle near first-stage burnout

Applying 294 kN to a 2 t vehicle would produce about 15 G, exceeding the 8 G limit.
The first stage must throttle near separation. With few nodes, its interpolated thrust oscillates,
and acceleration can exceed 8 G **between** nodes even though the node constraints hold.

### A long coast and Hohmann-like transfer

The second stage accelerates near 100–150 km altitude, then coasts around Earth to 500 km apogee.
Insertion occurs <Value id="rocket_ascent_polar_tsto" k="downrange_km" d={0} /> km downrange.
A final burn circularizes the orbit. This resembles a **Hohmann transfer**: accelerate low, coast outward, and circularize at apogee.
The low-thrust upper stage produces longer burns, with a coast exceeding 30 minutes in this solution.

### Why staging helps

Discarding roughly 1 t of first-stage structure leaves a lighter vehicle for the second-stage engine,
which is also more efficient in near-vacuum conditions.
Unlike Example 12's very optimistic 1% dry mass, staging permits less extreme structural assumptions.

## Try it

- Raise stage-2 `Isp` from 350 to 450 and compare delivered mass and burn duration.
- Relax `MaxG` from 8 to 15 and inspect throttling and oscillations near separation.
- Increase `[20, 20]` to `[30, 30]` nodes and compare thrust oscillation.
