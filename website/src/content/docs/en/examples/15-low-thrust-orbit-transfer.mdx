<p class="lead">
  Electric propulsion uses propellant efficiently but produces very little thrust compared with gravity.
  A spacecraft must gradually expand its orbit over several revolutions. When and in which direction should it thrust to save fuel?

## Situation

Transfer from a circular orbit of radius 1 to one of radius 4.
Each thrust-acceleration component is limited to 0.01, only 1% of gravity at the initial orbit.
Length and velocity units are the initial radius and circular speed, so the gravitational parameter is $\mu=1$.
Initial and final orbital periods are $2\pi\approx6.28$ and $16\pi\approx50.3$.
The time limit 55 equals about 8.8 initial orbital periods.

Thrust is expressed as **acceleration**, and spacecraft mass change is neglected.
The objective is a componentwise accumulated velocity change, used as a propellant-use measure.

## Formulation

### Coordinates

Radius $r$ is distance from the central body, $v_r$ is outward velocity, and $v_t$ is velocity along the orbital direction.
The thrust accelerations are $u_r$ and $u_t$.
Angle does not enter the dynamics, so it is omitted from the states and recovered for plotting by integrating $\dot\theta=v_t/r$.

### Split positive and negative controls

$$
u_r=u_r^+-u_r^-,\qquad u_t=u_t^+-u_t^-,\qquad u_r^\pm,u_t^\pm\ge0.
$$

The positive radial component points outward; the negative one inward.
Tangential components point along or against the direction of travel.

### Dynamics

$$
\dot r=v_r,\qquad
\dot v_r=\frac{v_t^2}r-\frac1{r^2}+u_r^+-u_r^-,\qquad
\dot v_t=-\frac{v_rv_t}r+u_t^+-u_t^-.
$$

Gravity and centrifugal acceleration balance for $v_t=1/\sqrt r$.
Without tangential thrust, angular momentum $rv_t$ is conserved, so outward motion reduces tangential speed.

### Boundary conditions

$$
r(0)=1,\quad v_r(0)=0,\quad v_t(0)=1,\qquad
r(t_f)=4,\quad v_r(t_f)=0,\quad v_t(t_f)=0.5.
$$

Both endpoints are circular orbits. Terminal angle is free.

### Constraints

$$
0\le u_r^+,u_r^-,u_t^+,u_t^-\le0.01,\qquad1\le r\le4,\qquad1\le t_f\le55.
$$

Limits apply to **components**, so simultaneous radial and tangential thrust can have magnitude $0.01\sqrt2$.
Radius bounds apply at interior nodes; endpoints are fixed by equalities.

### Objective

$$
J=\int_0^{t_f}(u_r^++u_r^-+u_t^++u_t^-)\,dt.
$$

Absolute value is not differentiable at zero. Splitting $u=u^+-u^-$ replaces $|u|$ by the smooth linear sum $u^++u^-$.
At the optimum, simultaneous opposing thrust only wastes cost, so one component is zero and the sum equals $|u|$.

The objective adds magnitudes **by direction**. It is an L1 measure, which exceeds the Euclidean thrust magnitude
$\sqrt{u_r^2+u_t^2}$ when both directions are active. It can represent separate thrusters for the two directions.

### Parameters and initial guess

Use $\mu=1$, $u_{\max}=0.01$, initial state $(1,0,1)$, target state $(4,0,0.5)$, and maximum time 55.
Guess final time 50, radius linear from 1 to 4, zero radial velocity, and tangential velocity $1/\sqrt r$.
Set $u_t^+=0.005$ and the other controls to zero.
The guess approximates a slow outward spiral. Sixty nodes resolve the long trajectory.

## Explanation

### Why forward thrust can accompany decreasing speed

Forward thrust raises orbital energy, expanding the orbit.
Circular speed is lower at larger radius, $v_t=1/\sqrt r$.
The reduction from outward motion, $-v_rv_t/r$, can exceed the acceleration from $u_t$.
Energy rises even as tangential speed falls.

### Time and fuel

A slow, nearly circular spiral has an ideal velocity-change requirement of $1-0.5=0.5$.
A two-impulse **Hohmann transfer** needs about 0.449.
The computed objective <Value id="low_thrust_orbit_transfer" k="objective_delta_v" d={3} /> is larger than both.

The active time limit forces near-continuous thrust to reach radius 4 by time 55, leaving little opportunity to coast.
A looser limit allows thrust to concentrate at more efficient orbital locations.

### Node resolution

The path covers roughly four revolutions. The angle is reconstructed for the scene, while one polynomial represents each state and control over the full interval.
Around thrust switches, interpolation can oscillate or exceed 0.01 between nodes because constraints apply only at nodes
([multiple phases](../../theory/pseudospectral/#multiple-phases)).

## Try it

- Raise `tf_max` from 55 to 70. A representative solve finishes around 65 with objective near 0.50 and several coast intervals.
- Change `rf` and `vtf` together to preserve a circular target: for `rf = 2`, use `vtf = 1/sqrt(2)` (about 0.707).
- Raise `u_max` to 0.02 and compare revolutions and the objective with the Hohmann value about 0.449.
