## Situation

Orbital ascent requires building height, then horizontal speed, and reaching the target with no vertical velocity.
Here we isolate the steering question:

- Motion is planar, without gravity or drag.
- Acceleration magnitude is fixed at $a=1$ m/s².
- The vehicle starts at rest and must finish at $y=1$ m with $u=1$ m/s and $v=0$.
- Final horizontal position is free.

Accelerating only upward gains height but leaves vertical speed and no horizontal speed.
Accelerating only sideways gains no height. Steering must meet both requirements together.

## Formulation

### Dynamics

$$
\dot u=a\cos\beta,\qquad\dot v=a\sin\beta,\qquad\dot x=u,\qquad\dot y=v.
$$

Angle $\beta=0$ points horizontally forward; $90^\circ$ points up; negative angles point downward.

### Boundary conditions

$$
u(0)=v(0)=x(0)=y(0)=0,\qquad u(t_f)=1,\quad v(t_f)=0,\quad y(t_f)=1.
$$

There is no condition on $x(t_f)$.

### Constraints, objective, and parameter

$$
-\frac\pi2\le\beta\le\frac\pi2,\qquad J=t_f,\qquad a=1\ \mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

The acceleration direction stays in the forward half-plane.

### Initial guess

For time 0 to 2 s, let $s=t/2$:

- $u=s$ and $x=s^2$.
- $y=3s^2-2s^3$ and $v=3s(1-s)$.
- $\beta=\arctan\bigl(3(1-2s)\bigr)$, turning from upward to downward.

## Explanation

### Gain height, then cancel vertical speed

Early acceleration points almost upward to build vertical speed. Later it points downward to cancel that speed exactly at $y=1$.
Horizontal speed grows most rapidly around the middle, when $\beta$ passes through zero.
The fixed acceleration is allocated between reaching the height target and reaching 1 m/s horizontally.
Without gravity, the upward and downward portions are nearly symmetric.

### The linear tangent law

For this problem, optimal steering has the form

$$
\tan\beta(t)=c_1-c_2t.
$$

This **linear tangent law** underlies practical rocket guidance.
The free final horizontal position makes the denominator constant in this case.
The computed $\tan\beta$ is almost linear, while $\beta$ itself forms an S-shaped curve, changing slowly near its endpoints and quickly near the middle.
The initial guess has the same general form.

[Indirect methods](../../theory/methods/) derive this property from optimality conditions.
OpenGoddard obtains it directly from the dynamics and constraints without requiring the steering law in advance.

## Try it

- Set `PARAMETERS["a"]` to 2 and compare duration, trajectory, and steering.
- Add a final horizontal-position equality. The tangent of the steering angle is no longer a linear function of time.
- Change the node count from 20 to 10 or 40 and compare the stable digits of the minimum duration.
