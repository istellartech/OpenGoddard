## Situation

A 100 t rocket starts at rest and must enter a **400 km circular orbit**.
At arrival it needs the target altitude, zero radial velocity, and circular speed about 7.67 km/s.
The dry vehicle is 1 t and propellant accounts for 99% of initial mass.
Maximizing final mass measures how much propellant can remain. Earth rotation is neglected.
Thrust and the acceleration felt by the vehicle are limited.

## Formulation

### Coordinates

- $R$ is distance from Earth's center; altitude is $h=R-R_e$.
- $\theta$ is angular distance from the launch site; downrange distance is $R_e\theta$.
- $V_r$ is outward radial (vertical) velocity; $V_t$ is tangential (local horizontal) velocity.
- $T_r,T_t$ are thrust components. Magnitude is $|T|=\sqrt{T_r^2+T_t^2}$ and elevation is $\arctan(T_r/T_t)$ in this nonnegative quadrant.

Components avoid introducing a steering angle that becomes undefined at zero thrust and adds trigonometric nonlinearities.

### Dynamics

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot R&=V_r, &\dot\theta&=V_t/R,\\
\dot V_r&=(T_r-D_r)/m-\mu/R^2+V_t^2/R,\\
\dot V_t&=(T_t-D_t)/m-V_rV_t/R,\\
\dot m&=-\sqrt{T_r^2+T_t^2}/(g_0I_{sp}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The centrifugal term $V_t^2/R$ balances gravity on a circular orbit.
The term $-V_rV_t/R$ expresses conservation of angular momentum: without tangential force, $RV_t$ stays constant as radius changes.

$$
V=\sqrt{V_r^2+V_t^2},\qquad
D_r=\tfrac12\rho(h)V_rVC_DA,\qquad D_t=\tfrac12\rho(h)V_tVC_DA,
$$

$$
\rho(h)=1.225e^{-h/8500},\qquad g(R)=\mu/R^2.
$$

Drag opposes velocity. The same gravitational parameter $\mu$ is used for the dynamics and target circular speed.

### Boundary conditions

$$
\begin{aligned}
R(0)&=R_e,&\theta(0)&=0,&V_r(0)&=V_t(0)=0,&m(0)&=100\ \mathrm t,\\
R(t_f)&=R_e+400\ \mathrm{km},&V_r(t_f)&=0,&V_t(t_f)&=\sqrt{\mu/R(t_f)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Target speed is about 7672.6 m/s. Final angle and time are free.

### Constraints

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&\ge R_e,\\
0&\le T_r,T_t\le T_{\max},\\
\sqrt{T_r^2+T_t^2}&\le T_{\max},\\
1\ \mathrm t&\le m\le100\ \mathrm t,\\
\left|\frac{\boldsymbol T-\boldsymbol D}{m}\right|&\le8g_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thrust cannot point downward or backward. Its magnitude is limited to 1.5 times initial weight, about 1471 kN.
The 8 G limit is **non-gravitational acceleration**, as measured by an accelerometer. All constraints apply at nodes.

### Objective and parameters

$$
J=-m(t_f)/m(0).
$$

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| $R_e$ | 6371 km |
| $g_0$ | 9.80665 m/s² |
| $\mu$ | $3.986\times10^{14}$ m³/s² |
| Initial / dry mass | 100 t / 1 t |
| $C_D,A$ | 0.6, 4 m² |
| $I_{sp}$ | 300 s |
| Thrust-to-weight ratio | 1.5 |
| Acceleration limit | 8 G |

Scale radius by $R_e$, speed by $\sqrt{\mu/R_e}$, and mass by initial mass
([scaling](../../guide/usage/#scaling)).

### Initial guess

Use time 0 to 200 s. Radius rises cubically to the target, angle rises cubically to 25°, radial velocity is zero,
and tangential velocity grows linearly to target speed.
Mass decreases cubically from 100 t to 1 t. Both thrust components decrease cubically from $T_{\max}/2$ to zero.

## Explanation

### Rise, then turn

Thrust starts nearly upward, around 80°, then turns toward horizontal.
Early climbing escapes dense air and drag. Turning too late wastes propellant against gravity.
The optimum balances these effects, resembling a gravity turn.

### Burn, coast, and circularize

The first burn creates a trajectory with apogee near 400 km. Coasting raises altitude without consuming propellant.
A final horizontal burn at apogee creates the circular orbit, similar to a **Hohmann transfer**.
Tangential speed decreases during the outward coast because angular momentum is conserved.

### Interpreting the plots

Constraints hold at nodes; interpolating curves may exceed limits between them.
A single polynomial spans both engine switches, so fine details depend on the node count
([multiple phases](../../theory/pseudospectral/#multiple-phases), [checking results](../../guide/troubleshooting/#check-the-result-first)).

## Try it

- Change the 30 nodes or the 200 s time guess and compare convergence and final mass.
- Lower `MaxG` from 8 to 4 and inspect throttling when the acceleration constraint becomes active.
- Change `Htarget` to 300 or 600 km and compare coast duration and final mass.
