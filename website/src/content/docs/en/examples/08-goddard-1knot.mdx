## Situation

The rocket and objective are the same as [Example 07](../07-goddard-0knot/).
Its thrust changes from maximum through an intermediate singular arc to zero.
The one-phase solution burns out around 0.077, so we place a **knot** at 0.075:

- Phase 1: $0\le t\le0.075$, primarily powered ascent.
- Phase 2: $0.075\le t\le t_f$, primarily coasting.

These roles are encouraged by the initial guess; the thrust constraints themselves are the same in both phases.
All quantities remain dimensionless.

## Formulation

### Dynamics

Both phases use

$$
\dot h=v,\qquad\dot v=\frac{T-D(h,v)}m-g(h),\qquad\dot m=-\frac Tc,
$$

$$
D=D_cv^2\exp\!\left(-H_c\frac{h-h_0}{h_0}\right),\qquad g=g_0\left(\frac{h_0}h\right)^2.
$$

See [Example 07](../07-goddard-0knot/#dynamics) for the meaning of each term.

### Phases and linkage

$$
t_1=0.075,\qquad
h^{(1)}(t_1)=h^{(2)}(t_1),\quad
v^{(1)}(t_1)=v^{(2)}(t_1),\quad
m^{(1)}(t_1)=m^{(2)}(t_1).
$$

States are continuous, but no thrust continuity condition is imposed. Thrust may jump at the knot.

### Boundary conditions

$$
h(0)=1,\quad v(0)=0,\quad m(0)=1,\qquad v(t_f)=0,\quad m(t_f)=0.6.
$$

### Constraints and objective

$$
0\le T\le3.5,\quad h\ge1,\quad v\ge0,\quad0.6\le m\le1,
$$

$$
t_1\ge0.05,\qquad t_f\ge0.1,\qquad J=-h(t_f).
$$

The knot lower bound is inactive because the equality already fixes $t_1$.

### Parameters

The parameters match Example 07: $h_0=g_0=m_0=1$, $D_c=310$, $H_c=500$, $c=0.5$,
$T_{\max}=3.5$, and $m_f=0.6$, with the additional knot time 0.075.

### Initial guess

Start with phase times $0\to0.1\to0.3$; optimization moves the knot to 0.075.
Height is cubic from 1 to 1.01 and velocity is zero.
Mass decreases linearly from 1 to 0.6 in phase 1, then remains 0.6.
Thrust starts at maximum throughout phase 1 and zero throughout phase 2.

## Explanation

### Why oscillations decrease

The discontinuity now lies **between** the final node of phase 1 and the first node of phase 2.
Within each phase, thrust is smooth enough for a polynomial. Phase 2 can represent zero thrust directly.
Phase 1 also has fewer demands on its polynomial, reducing oscillation around the maximum-to-intermediate transition.

### Choosing the knot time

This example **fixes** the knot based on the previous solution.
It is slightly earlier than the one-phase burnout estimate <Value id="goddard_0knot" k="burnout_time" d={3} />,
which makes the rocket consume propellant earlier and slightly reduces final height, around the fifth decimal place.
The late rise in phase-1 thrust is consistent with burning the remaining propellant before the fixed knot.

Removing $t_1=0.075$ allows the optimizer to choose the knot.
Additional conditions, such as consuming propellant by the end of phase 1, may be needed to keep it aligned with burnout.

## Try it

- Change `result.equal(ts0, 0.075)` to 0.06 or 0.09. An earlier knot may reduce height; a later one may put the thrust jump inside phase 1 and restore oscillations.
- Reduce `[25, 25]` to `[15, 10]` and compare accuracy with the 50-node one-phase solution.
- Remove the knot-time equality and inspect the optimized knot and thrust history.
