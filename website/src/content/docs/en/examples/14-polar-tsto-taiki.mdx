## Situation

Launch a 1.8 m diameter rocket from Taiki, Hokkaido (about 42.5° N), carrying a 100 kg payload to a 561 km circular orbit at 96.7° inclination.
This inclination is slightly retrograde, a type used for Sun-synchronous Earth-observation orbits.
Compared with [Example 13](../13-rocket-ascent-polar-tsto/), the model adds:

- **Atmosphere tables:** linearly interpolated density, pressure, and sound speed from the US Standard Atmosphere between 0 and 86 km. Density and pressure are zero above 86 km.
- **Mach-dependent drag:** a coefficient table with a transonic peak.
- **Earth rotation:** the launch site already has velocity in an inertial frame.
- **Ambient-pressure corrections:** thrust and specific impulse improve as pressure decreases.

## Formulation

### Coordinates

Use the [polar coordinates of Example 12](../12-rocket-ascent-polar-ssto/#coordinates).
Velocities are measured in a frame that does not rotate with Earth.
This is a planar approximation, rather than a full three-dimensional inclination model.

### Phases and linkage

At optimized separation time $t_s$,

$$
R(t_s^+)=R(t_s^-),\quad\theta(t_s^+)=\theta(t_s^-),\quad
V_r(t_s^+)=V_r(t_s^-),\quad V_t(t_s^+)=V_t(t_s^-),\quad m(t_s^+)=m_2.
$$

Position and velocity remain continuous; mass resets to the initial second-stage mass.

### Dynamics

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot R&=V_r,&\dot\theta&=V_t/R,\\
\dot V_r&=(T_r-D_r)/m-\mu/R^2+V_t^2/R,\\
\dot V_t&=(T_t-D_t)/m-V_rV_t/R,\\
\dot m&=-\sqrt{T_r^2+T_t^2}/\{g_0I_{sp,i}(h)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The centrifugal and angular-momentum terms are unchanged from Example 13.
The same $\mu$ defines gravity and target circular speed.

### Atmosphere and drag

Density $\rho(h)$, pressure $p(h)$, and sound speed $a(h)$ are interpolated from tables:

$$
V=\sqrt{V_r^2+V_t^2},\qquad M=V/a(h),
$$

$$
D_r=\tfrac12\rho(h)V_rVC_D(M)A,\qquad
D_t=\tfrac12\rho(h)V_tVC_D(M)A.
$$

The drag coefficient is about 0.28 at subsonic speeds, peaks near 0.68 around Mach 1.2–1.4,
and falls to about 0.34 near Mach 4.5. Values outside the table are linearly extrapolated.
The 1.8 m diameter gives area $A\approx2.54$ m².

### Pressure-dependent thrust and specific impulse

Reference performance is sea-level for stage 1 (261 s) and vacuum for stage 2 (322 s).
With nozzle exit areas $A_{e,i}$ and launch altitude $h_0=10$ m,

$$
\Delta T_1(h)=\{p(h_0)-p(h)\}A_{e,1},\qquad
\Delta T_2(h)=-p(h)A_{e,2}.
$$

`Rocket.delta_thrust` computes these corrections. Specific impulse changes consistently:

$$
\dot m_{\mathrm{ref},i}=\frac{T_{\max,i}}{g_0I_{sp,i}},\qquad
I_{sp,i}(h)=I_{sp,i}+\frac{\Delta T_i(h)}{g_0\dot m_{\mathrm{ref},i}}.
$$

The thrust limit becomes $T_{\max,i}+\Delta T_i(h)$.
At that limit, mass flow remains $\dot m_{\mathrm{ref},i}$ regardless of altitude: lower external pressure gives more thrust at the same propellant flow.
For stage 1, the vacuum correction adds about 41.6 kN and raises specific impulse to about 294 s.
Stage 2 operates in nearly zero pressure, so its correction is small.

### Earth rotation and initial velocity

At latitude $\phi$, the launch site's eastward speed is

$$
v_{\mathrm{site}}=\omega R_e\cos\phi\approx342.5\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$

For orbit inclination $i$, launch azimuth $A_z$ satisfies $\sin A_z=\cos i/\cos\phi$.
Projecting rotation into the modeled direction gives

$$
V_0=v_{\mathrm{site}}\sin A_z=\omega R_e\cos i\approx-54.2\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$

Latitude cancels in this projection. The retrograde target requires a slightly westward azimuth, about −9° from north,
so rotation opposes the modeled flight direction.
The cross-plane component, about 338 m/s, is omitted by the two-dimensional model.
Drag uses the modeled velocity directly; atmospheric co-rotation is not included.

### Boundary conditions

$$
\begin{aligned}
R(0)&=R_e+10\ \mathrm m,&\theta(0)&=0,\\
V_r(0)&=0,&V_t(0)&=V_0,&m(0)&=m_1+m_2,\\
R(t_f)&=R_e+561\ \mathrm{km},&V_r(t_f)&=0,&V_t(t_f)&=\sqrt{\mu/R(t_f)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Stage masses are $m_1=13000$ kg and $m_2=1566.7$ kg including payload, giving launch mass 14566.7 kg.
Target speed is about 7583.0 m/s.

### Constraints

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&\ge R_e,\\
m_{d,1}+m_2\le m\le m_1+m_2&\quad\text{(phase 1)},\\
m_{d,2}+m_{\mathrm{pl}}\le m\le m_2&\quad\text{(phase 2)},\\
\sqrt{T_r^2+T_t^2}&\le T_{\max,i}+\Delta T_i(h),\\
q=\tfrac12\rho V^2&\le500\ \mathrm{kPa},\\
\left|\frac{\boldsymbol T-\boldsymbol D}{m}\right|&\le20g_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The mass lower bounds are 2866.7 kg for phase 1 and 320 kg (220 kg structure + 100 kg payload) for phase 2.
Thrust direction is free. Dynamic pressure measures aerodynamic loading; its 500 kPa limit is generous.
Acceleration excludes gravity.

### Objective and parameters

$$
J=-m(t_f)/(13000\ \mathrm{kg}).
$$

| Parameter | Stage 1 | Stage 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dry mass | 1300 kg | 220 kg |
| Structural fraction | 10% | 15% |
| Wet stage mass, excluding payload | 13000 kg | 1466.7 kg |
| Payload | — | 100 kg |
| Reference maximum thrust | 33.3 tf (326.6 kN) | 4.2 tf (41.2 kN) |
| Reference specific impulse | 261 s, sea level | 322 s, vacuum |
| Engines / nozzle expansion ratio | 9 / 20 | 1 / 140 |
| Total nozzle exit area | 0.411 m² | 0.319 m² |

The throat diameter is 53.9 mm. Use $R_e=6371$ km, $g_0=9.80665$ m/s², $\mu=3.986\times10^{14}$ m³/s²,
latitude 42.506°, rotation rate $7.2921159\times10^{-5}$ rad/s, and inclination 96.7°.
Scaling follows Examples 12–13 with mass unit 13000 kg.

### Initial guess

Use times $0\to200\to800$ s. Radius and angle increase cubically to target altitude and 25°.
Radial velocity is zero; tangential velocity grows linearly from $V_0$ to target.
Mass decreases cubically to the phase lower bounds.
Thrust components decrease cubically from $0.9T_{\max,i}$ radially and $0.1T_{\max,i}$ tangentially to zero.

## Explanation

### The trajectory structure remains similar

The vehicle rises, turns horizontal, accelerates with the upper stage, coasts to target apogee, and circularizes.
More detailed atmosphere and engine models change the numbers while retaining this Hohmann-like structure.
Simple models help identify the pattern; detailed ones refine the estimates.

### What changes with a detailed model?

- Drag coefficient increases about 2.4-fold near sonic speed. Dynamic pressure peaks early, then falls with density.
- Rotation costs about 54 m/s in this westward projection; an eastward target could benefit instead.
- First-stage thrust and specific impulse increase with altitude. Nodes above the reference red thrust line can therefore remain feasible.

### First-stage thrust zigzags

The solver reports convergence, but first-stage thrust varies strongly between nodes.
Final mass appears relatively insensitive to the detailed timing of first-stage thrust, so do not interpret each oscillation as a meaningful flight feature.
More nodes or thrust-rate constraints can improve the representation.

## Try it

- Lower `MaxQ` from 500000 to 30000 Pa. Inspect the clipped dynamic-pressure peak and reduction in final mass.
- Change `inclination` from 96.7° to 45°. The projected rotational initial speed becomes positive; compare delivered mass.
- Raise `Mpayload` from 100 to 150 kg and check whether final mass still exceeds dry mass plus payload.
