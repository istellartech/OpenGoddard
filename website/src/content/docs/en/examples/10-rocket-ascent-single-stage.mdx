## Situation

The rocket flies vertically from the surface, burning propellant and becoming lighter.
Drag grows with speed but decreases with atmospheric density. Gravity also decreases with altitude.
As in [Example 07](../07-goddard-0knot/), maximum thrust throughout is not necessarily optimal.
Here all equations use physical units.

## Formulation

### Dynamics

$$
\dot R=v,\qquad\dot v=\frac{T-D}m-\frac{GM}{R^2},\qquad
\dot m=-\frac{T}{g_0I_{sp}},
$$

$$
D=\tfrac12\rho(R-R_e)v^2C_dA,\qquad\rho(h)=\rho_0e^{-h/H}.
$$

$R$ is distance from Earth's center; altitude is $h=R-R_e$.
**Specific impulse** $I_{sp}$ measures engine efficiency; $g_0I_{sp}$ is effective exhaust velocity.
Larger $I_{sp}$ uses less propellant for the same thrust.
Atmospheric density falls by a factor $1/e$ per scale height $H=8500$ m.

### Boundary conditions

$$
R(0)=R_e,\quad v(0)=0,\quad m(0)=5000\ \mathrm{kg},\qquad
v(t_f)=0,\quad m(t_f)=2000\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

The endpoint is the highest point after consuming all 3000 kg of propellant.

### Constraints and objective

$$
0\le T\le T_{\max},\quad R\ge R_e,\quad v\ge0,\quad
2000\le m\le5000\ \mathrm{kg},\quad t_f\ge10\ \mathrm s,
$$

$$
T_{\max}=2m(0)g_0=98066.5\ \mathrm N,\qquad J=-\frac{R(t_f)}{R_e}.
$$

Dividing by Earth radius scales the objective without changing its optimum.

### Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $GM$ | Earth gravitational parameter | $3.986004418\times10^{14}$ m³/s² |
| $R_e$ | Earth radius | 6371 km |
| $g_0$ | Standard gravity | 9.80665 m/s² |
| $m_0,m_f$ | Initial and dry masses | 5000, 2000 kg |
| $C_d,A$ | Drag coefficient and area | 0.2, 10 m² |
| $I_{sp}$ | Specific impulse | 300 s |
| $\rho_0,H$ | Surface density and scale height | 1.225 kg/m³, 8500 m |

At maximum thrust, consumption is about 33.3 kg/s, so all propellant would be exhausted in 90 s.

### Scaling

Use radius unit $R_e$, velocity unit $\sqrt{GM/R_e}\approx7.91$ km/s, mass unit 5000 kg,
time unit about 805 s, and thrust unit about 49.1 kN.
Equations and output stay in physical units ([scaling](../../guide/usage/#scaling)).

### Initial guess

Use time 0 to 600 s, cubic radius from $R_e$ to $R_e+50$ km with zero endpoint slopes, and zero velocity.
Mass is cubic from 5000 to 2000 kg, with initial slope −0.6 kg/s and final slope zero.
Thrust decreases cubically from maximum to zero with zero endpoint slopes.

## Explanation

For about the first 20 s, maximum thrust builds speed. As drag grows, thrust falls to roughly 70–80 kN,
balancing drag and gravity losses along a **singular arc**.
Once the atmosphere thins, the rocket consumes the remaining propellant and coasts upward.
Throttling extends the burn beyond the 90 s maximum-thrust duration.
The physical structure matches the dimensionless Goddard problem.

Thrust oscillations and a rounded burnout transition arise from representing a discontinuity with one polynomial.
Splitting near burnout, as in Example 08, can improve this representation
([multiple phases](../../theory/pseudospectral/#multiple-phases)).

## Try it

- Set `self.Cd` to zero and compare the singular arc and maximum altitude with the drag-free result.
- Raise `self.Isp` from 300 to 450 and inspect the increase in altitude.
- Lower `self.max_thrust`, the initial thrust-to-weight ratio, to 1.5 and compare maximum-thrust and intermediate-thrust durations.
