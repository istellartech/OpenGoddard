## Situation

As in [Example 03](../03-brachistochrone/), a ball slides without friction under gravity.
Here the endpoint is fixed at $(1,0.2)$ m, and a **floor** limits depth to 0.3 m.
Without the floor, the fastest path descends below the destination, then climbs back up.
We ask how the floor changes that solution. Gravity is 9.81 m/s².

## Formulation

### Dynamics

Depth is positive downward. Angle $0$ points down, $90^\circ$ is horizontal, and angles above $90^\circ$ climb upward:

$$
\dot{x}=v\sin\theta,\qquad\dot{y}=v\cos\theta,\qquad\dot{v}=g\cos\theta.
$$

On the uphill segment, $\cos\theta<0$ and the ball slows down. Energy conservation still gives $v=\sqrt{2gy}$.

### Boundary conditions

$$
x(0)=y(0)=v(0)=0,\qquad x(t_f)=1,\quad y(t_f)=0.2.
$$

Terminal speed is not prescribed, but energy determines it as $\sqrt{2\cdot9.81\cdot0.2}\approx1.98$ m/s.

### Constraints

$$
0\le y\le0.3,\qquad v\ge0,\qquad0\le\theta\le\pi,\qquad0.01\le t_f\le5.
$$

The depth upper bound is the floor. Bounds apply at **every node** and therefore act as path constraints.
The other bounds keep optimization within the intended physical range.

### Objective and parameters

$$
J=t_f.
$$

| Symbol | Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $g$ | `gravity` | 9.81 m/s² |
| $l$ | `distance` | 1 m |
| $y_f$ | `final_depth` | 0.2 m |
| $y_{\max}$ | `max_depth` | 0.3 m |

### Initial guess

For time 0 to 0.7 s, let $s=t/0.7$ and set

$$
x=ls,\qquad y=y_fs+0.2\sin(\pi s),\qquad v=\sqrt{2gy}.
$$

The depth bulges downward and returns to the target. Angle increases linearly from 0 to 2 rad (about 115°).

## Explanation

### The boundary arc

The floor blocks the deepest, fastest part of the unconstrained curve.
The next best choice is to spend time at the greatest allowed depth, where speed reaches $\sqrt{2gy_{\max}}\approx2.43$ m/s.
A segment that remains on an active constraint, $y=y_{\max}$, is a **boundary arc**.
Its control is fixed by the constraint: $\theta=90^\circ$ keeps depth constant.
Cycloid segments join this arc tangentially on entry and exit.

### Why the time penalty is small

The floor excludes only a short part of the unconstrained path. Speed along the floor is still high,
so the arrival-time increase is less than 1%.

### Terminal direction

Unlike Example 03, depth is fixed at the endpoint. The ball must climb from the floor to 0.2 m,
so it arrives on an uphill segment rather than horizontally.

### Limits of one polynomial

The rate of change of angle changes abruptly at the ends of the boundary arc.
One polynomial rounds these corners. Since bounds hold only at nodes, interpolation can exceed the floor slightly,
by around $10^{-5}$ m. More nodes make the corners clearer.

## Try it

- Set `max_depth` to 0.4. The floor lies below the unconstrained maximum depth (about 0.358 m), becomes inactive, and duration approaches 0.6206 s.
- Set it to 0.25. The boundary arc becomes longer and duration rises to about 0.636 s.
- Use `solve(nodes=20)` and compare the angle near the boundary-arc transitions with the 40-node result.
