## Situation

This thought experiment extends [Example 03](../03-brachistochrone/) to a horizontal distance of 600 km.
The endpoints are at the **same height**, so conservation of energy gives zero arrival speed.
We simplify the Earth to a flat surface with constant gravity 9.8 m/s², neglecting curvature, changing gravity at depth, friction, and drag.

Distances are hundreds of thousands of meters, time hundreds of seconds, and angles around one radian.
These different magnitudes make **scaling** essential.

## Formulation

### Dynamics

Depth is positive downward; $\theta=90^\circ$ is horizontal, and larger angles climb:

$$
\dot{x}=v\sin\theta,\qquad\dot{y}=v\cos\theta,\qquad\dot{v}=g\cos\theta.
$$

Gravity's component along the tunnel changes speed. Energy gives $v=\sqrt{2gy}$.

### Boundary conditions

$$
x(0)=y(0)=v(0)=0,\qquad x(t_f)=l,\quad y(t_f)=0.
$$

The vehicle starts at rest at the surface in Tokyo and reaches the surface in Osaka, $l=600$ km away.

### Constraints and objective

$$
0\le x\le l,\qquad y\ge0,\qquad0\le\theta\le\pi,\qquad J=t_f.
$$

The trajectory stays between the cities, below ground, and does not reverse horizontally.
These bounds restrict the search to the intended region.

### Parameters

| Symbol | Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $g$ | `PARAMETERS["g"]` | 9.8 m/s² |
| $l$ | `PARAMETERS["l"]` | 600 000 m |
| $h$ | `PARAMETERS["h"]`, guessed maximum depth | 300 000 m |

### Scaling

| Variable | Unit | Approximate internal range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $x$ | 300 000 m | 0–2 |
| $y$ | 100 000 m | 0–1.9 |
| $v$ | 3000 m/s | 0–0.65 |
| $\theta$ | 1 rad | 0–3.1 |
| $t$ | 100 s | 0–6.2 |

The optimizer uses values divided by these units. Equations, conditions, and results remain in physical units.
Scaling changes numerical behavior **without changing the mathematical answer** ([scaling](../../guide/usage/#scaling)).
The velocity unit equals distance unit divided by time unit to balance the dynamics.

### Initial guess

Use time 0 to 600 s. Horizontal position increases linearly from 0 to 600 km.
Depth is V-shaped: the first 15 nodes descend to 300 km and the last 15 rise back to zero.
Angle increases linearly from 0 to $\pi$; speed starts at zero at all nodes.

## Explanation

### A complete cycloid arch

The cycloid is

$$
x=a(\phi-\sin\phi),\qquad y=a(1-\cos\phi).
$$

Unlike Example 03, the endpoint returns to the original height. The trajectory spans $\phi=0$ to $2\pi$,
so $a=l/(2\pi)\approx95.5$ km. Therefore

$$
y_{\max}=\frac{l}{\pi}\approx191\ \text{km},\qquad
 t_f=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi l}{g}},\qquad v_{\max}=\sqrt{2gy_{\max}}.
$$

Duration is proportional to $\sqrt l$: quadrupling distance only doubles time because the longer tunnel is deeper and allows higher speeds.

### Accuracy and scaling

With 30 nodes, duration differs from the analytic result by about 0.04 s (less than 0.01%), and maximum depth by about 1 km.
Without scaling, the optimizer must handle distances up to 600 000 alongside angles near 3, making its search poorly balanced.
With representative units, all variables have comparable magnitudes.

## Try it

- Set `l` to 400000 (roughly Tokyo to Nagoya). Expect duration about 506 s and maximum depth about 127 km.
- Set `unit_x`, `unit_y`, and `unit_t` to 1 in `make_problem`. The mathematical answer is unchanged, but the solver may fail with `Inequality constraints incompatible`.
- Change guessed maximum depth `h` to 150000 or 500000. Compare convergence to durations near 620.2 s, differing by only about 0.03 s.
