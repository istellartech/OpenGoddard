## Situation

We choose the point's velocity $u$ at every instant. This input is the **control**; position $x$, determined by that choice, is the **state**
([terminology](../../theory/optimal-control/)). We want to move from $x=0$ to $x=2$ while balancing two goals:

- Arrive sooner.
- Use less effort, measured by the integral of $u^2$.

All quantities are **dimensionless**. The purpose is to learn the structure of an optimal control problem.

## Formulation

### Dynamics

$$
\dot{x}=u.
$$

Position changes at the chosen velocity. This relationship must hold throughout optimization.

### Boundary conditions

$$
x(t_0)=0,\qquad x(t_f)=2.
$$

The start time is fixed at $t_0=5$ to demonstrate that time need not start at zero.
The optimal duration does not depend on this offset. The final time is free.

### Constraints

$$
0\le u\le3,\qquad 5.1\le t_f\le15.
$$

Duration therefore lies between 0.1 and 10. Neither bound is active at the optimum.

### Objective

$$
J=\underbrace{(t_f-t_0)}_{\text{duration}}+\underbrace{\int_{t_0}^{t_f}u^2\,dt}_{\text{control effort}}.
$$

The first term is a **terminal cost**; the second is a **running cost**.
Reducing duration requires faster motion and increases the running cost.

### Parameters and scaling

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $d$ | Distance (`target`) | 2 |
| $t_0$ | Start time | 5 |
| $u_{\max}$ | Velocity limit | 3 |

The time and position units are 3 and 2 internally ([scaling](../../guide/usage/#scaling)). Results are returned in the original units.

### Initial guess

Start with time 5 to 8, position increasing linearly from 0 to 2, and constant velocity $2/3$.

## Explanation

### Best motion for a fixed duration

Let $T=t_f-t_0$. The mean velocity is $d/T$.
For a given mean, **constant velocity minimizes the integral of the square**: the mean square is at least the square of the mean.
Thus $u=d/T$ and

$$
\int_{t_0}^{t_f}u^2\,dt=\left(\frac{d}{T}\right)^2T=\frac{d^2}{T}.
$$

### Best duration

$$
J(T)=T+\frac{d^2}{T}.
$$

Setting $dJ/dT=1-d^2/T^2=0$ gives

$$
T=d=2,\qquad u=d/T=1,\qquad J=2d=4.
$$

The computed duration <Value id="minimum_energy_transfer" k="duration" d={4} /> and objective <Value id="minimum_energy_transfer" k="objective" d={4} /> agree.
For this particular objective, the optimal speed is **1 regardless of distance**, provided the bounds remain inactive. Only duration changes.

### What the numerical method does

OpenGoddard optimizes position and velocity at eight nodes, plus final time, subject to dynamics and constraints
([direct methods](../../theory/methods/), [pseudospectral methods](../../theory/pseudospectral/)).
The solution is linear in position and constant in velocity, so even eight nodes represent it exactly.

## Try it

- Change `target = 2.0` in `main()` to 1 or 5. Duration should equal distance and velocity should remain 1.
- Set `target` to 20. The unconstrained duration 20 exceeds the allowed duration 10, so the optimum has duration 10, velocity 2, and cost $10+20^2/10=50$.
- Change the node count in `Problem([5, 8], [8], ...)` to 3 or 20 and compare the answer.
